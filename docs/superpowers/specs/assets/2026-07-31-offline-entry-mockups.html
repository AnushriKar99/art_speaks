A · Tap the photo

4 taps total. Tap a product to add one; tap again for two. Badge shows the count. Long-press removes.

You recognise your own work by sight instantly — no reading, no searching. Price auto-fills from the product but stays editable for market discounts.

B · Search and add

~20 interactions. Type part of a name, tap the match, adjust quantity, repeat for each product.

Scales to hundreds of products and is the familiar pattern. But it's typing on a phone, possibly outdoors, one product at a time — exactly the hassle you want to avoid.

C · Scrolling tally sheet

4 taps — plus hunting through the list.

No typing, and it doubles as an end-of-market stock check. But it degrades as your catalogue grows: at 48 products you're scrolling past 44 zeroes to reach the 4 that sold.

My recommendation

A, with B's search bar bolted on top. The photo grid handles the everyday case in 4 taps; a search field above it stops the grid becoming a scroll-hunt once you pass ~40 products. That's one screen that stays fast at every catalogue size.

Whichever you pick, the date defaults to today but is one tap to change — you'll often enter Saturday's market on Sunday morning. And each tile shows current stock, so a tile reading 0 left that you just sold two of tells you your count has drifted.
